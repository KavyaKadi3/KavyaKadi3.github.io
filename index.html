01 The through-line

Every job I've had started with the same problem wearing a different costume: a pile of information only humans could interpret — and never enough humans.

At a bird-conservation nonprofit, it was photographs of glass towers, with volunteers eyeballing façades to guess collision risk. I trained a PyTorch vision model to make the first pass. At AMD, it was firmware logs during MI300 silicon bring-up — engineers triaging regressions by hand until my Airflow pipelines started surfacing failures on their own. At ADP, it was tax-jurisdiction rules scattered across a payroll platform, kept in sync by code I wrote instead of by people. Now at Manulife, it's investment documents: multi-agent AI workflows that extract, validate, and route what analysts used to retype.

That pattern taught me what to optimize for — not clever models, but boring reliability. Clean APIs. Event-driven backends. Pipelines that still work at 2 a.m. The interesting part of applied AI isn't the demo; it's the unglamorous last mile that makes it dependable.

The foundation: computer engineering at the University of Toronto, with minors in AI and engineering business — where I also built a public-health analytics dashboard for researchers under Dr. Yu Chen.

02 Where the pattern played out

  1. 2024 — now

    Manulife · Software Engineer, Investments AI

    Documents in, decisions out. I build the production side of document intelligence for investment operations: multi-agent workflows that read and reconcile filings, validation APIs that keep the outputs honest, and event-driven backends and migration tooling that move it all to cloud scale. The measure of success is simple — analysts stop retyping, and start reviewing.

  2. 2023

    ADP · Software Engineering Intern, Lifion Payroll

    Payroll is law compiled into software, and the law keeps changing. I built Node.js and SQL capabilities that automated tax-jurisdiction synchronization across the platform — replacing a manual, error-prone process with one that keeps millions of paychecks aligned with the rules by itself.

  3. 2022 — 23

    AMD · Software Engineering Intern, Data Center GPU (MI300)

    During silicon bring-up, every hour of triage is an hour of chip schedule. My Python automation and Airflow pipelines took over firmware regression and performance validation, flagging failures as they happened instead of after an engineer went digging — faster issue detection on one of AMD's flagship accelerators.

  4. 2020 — 21

    FLAP Canada · Machine Learning Intern

    Millions of birds die in window collisions every year, and risk assessment meant a human studying photos of buildings. I trained and shipped a computer-vision pipeline that estimates collision risk straight from an image — folding what was a manual review step into FLAP's mobile workflow.
